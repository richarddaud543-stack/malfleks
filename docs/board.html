The product

One question, asked at every branch

resolveDeliveryRoute() is four lines long and everything else is downstream of it.

Yes → brand fleet 6 of 19

  • The brand carries it. No courier, no third-party handoff.
  • No published ETA — brands don't quote one, so we don't invent one.

No → local courier 13 of 19

  • Couriers serving that branch's city are matched in.
  • Couriers do publish an ETA, so the estimate shown is theirs.
  • 5 couriers across Dar es Salaam, Arusha, Mwanza and Dodoma.

Why the answer arrives before the click

The route used to be computed one page after the customer had chosen. previewDelivery() now runs the same rule on the search card and the product page. Deliberate non-goal: no delivery-speed filter. Only couriers publish an ETA, so filtering on speed would silently hide every brand-fleet listing. Show the number; don't filter on one we only have half of.

The business · designed, not built

Brand → carrier → customer design

Everything from here to the roadmap is a decision, not code. It is written up in docs/marketplace-model.md. Phase 2 is the only one Soko actually operates — and that is exactly where the money comes from.

Phase 1

The brand branch

Owns the goods. Receives the order for the product it listed, and confirms it is available at that price. Gets paid at the door, less Soko's 6%.

Phase 2 · the product

Whoever carries it

Phase 3

The customer

Pays a deposit at order time by mobile money, then the rest in cash at the door. Never pays Soko twice, and never pays Soko for the goods.

Supply · designed, not built

Nobody signs up on the website design

Branches and couriers are real partners — the signup desk just lives off the site. That distinction is what makes the commission collectable: a pure directory could never charge for a sale it cannot see.

A branch or courier applies off-site. No self-serve form on the customer-facing website.

Step 4Admin reviews

Only now does the product appear to customers — and orders route back to that exact branch.

Once branches submit and admin publishes, the database becomes the source of truth and src/data drops to seed and demo fixtures.

Revenue · designed, not built

The deposit is exactly Soko's cut design

This is the load-bearing decision. The customer's upfront deposit equals Soko's total commission — nothing more. Everything else settles at the door between the customer, the branch and the courier, and Soko never touches it.

The platform fee is a ladder, not a percentage

The catalogue runs from a TZS 2,000 item to a TZS 245,000,000 SUV — 122,500× apart. At a flat 6% the small item yields 120 TZS (less than it costs to process) and the SUV yields 14,700,000 (no dealer would accept it). Both ends are real: 12 products sit under 100K, 18 sit above 10M. So the ladder applies to the goods payment, and the customer pays it as the deposit.

The first two rungs (highlighted) are the founder's own. The rest deliberately flatten — a ladder whose steps keep the same slope is just a flat percentage wearing a staircase costume: extending 500-per-5,000 all the way up is a constant 10% and a 24,500,000 TZS fee on the SUV, worse than the 6% it replaces.

What the 40,000 cap does to the business — read before changing it

On this catalogue the cap cuts fee revenue by 95%: if all 38 products sold once, a 1,200,000 ceiling earns 17,701,500 TZS and this one earns 898,500. That is not a mistake, it is a change of business model. Soko stops being a commission business (revenue scales with order value) and becomes a transaction-fee business (revenue scales with order count). A car sale earns 40,000 TZS — about seven earbud sales. The upside is the reason to do it: at 0.016%, Toyota and BMW have no commercial reason to refuse to list, where a 14,700,000 commission would have ended the conversation. It is land-grab pricing, and it makes volume the whole game.

Why this and not escrow load-bearing

  • Soko is paid before it does anything, and never chases a branch or courier.
  • No Bank of Tanzania licence. Soko is a merchant of an aggregator (Selcom, Azampay) — they hold the licence. The Act licenses whoever operates the payment system.
  • Cash on delivery, which still dominates the market, keeps working untouched.
  • The deposit doubles as the low-intent filter the research says the industry uses: someone who won't send 19,000 was never going to accept the fridge.

The courier ledger, and the real lever

  • Delivery money never passes through Soko at all — the customer pays the courier at the door, and the courier owes Soko its rung afterwards.
  • They settle it on the page they registered on. Goods revenue is secured; delivery revenue is a receivable — small, 6–8% per order, but it is credit.
  • The enforcement lever is job allocation, not invoicing — an overdue courier stops being offered by resolveDeliveryRoute().
  • That has to be a stated rule with a grace period, visible on their page, or it reads as arbitrary.

Refunds — the deposit is taken before anyone has done anything

Branch declines — out of stock, wrong priceFull refund. Soko did not deliver a customer; keeping it would be indefensible.
Customer cancels before dispatchFull refund inside a short window, then non-refundable.
Customer refuses at the doorNon-refundable — this is exactly the behaviour the deposit exists to discourage, and a driver has already made the trip.
Courier fails to deliverFull refund, and it counts against the courier.

Build order · designed, not built

Nothing is honest until the order is real design

Each stage ships on its own. Do not start a later one early — every stage after the first is dishonest without a durable order record behind it.

Stage 1

Make the order real prerequisite

Today createOrderRequest() builds the order in the browser, puts it in sessionStorage and sends it nowhere — the confirmation screen says so in plain words. Needs Order and OrderEvent in Prisma, a tested status machine (requested → confirmed → deposit_paid → dispatched → delivered → completed, plus declined/cancelled/refunded), and the server re-deriving every number from the catalogue.

Stage 2

Partners and admin stated first priority

Partner (branch or agent; applied / approved / suspended), PartnerUser mapping a Firebase uid to a partner, an admin role as a custom claim checked in the DAL, ProductSubmission plus /admin to review and publish, and /partner where a branch sees its orders and a courier sees its balance.

Stage 3

Money

An aggregator merchant account (your action, not code). A pure fee module — goods, delivery and route in; deposit, payouts and Soko's cut out — tested the way buildListings is, because money bugs are the worst kind. Integer TZS throughout with an explicit rounding rule. Deposit collected by mobile-money push to the phone already on the order.

Stage 4

Settlement and trust

Per-partner statements, delivery confirmation by PIN against the reference — which is also the evidence a dispute is judged on — and refund handling against the policy above.

One recorded decision has to change

docs/backend.md scopes Firestore to user-scoped data with no joins, which is why saved order requests went there. A real order is the opposite — it joins a product, a branch, a courier, a customer and a payment, and admin must query across customers. So orders go in Prisma and the Firestore saved-requests feature is superseded. That is a change to decision #14 and belongs in backend.md with its reasoning, not done quietly. Debts #20 and #21 move with it — the PII retention question doesn't disappear, it relocates.

Working agreement

No assignment begins cold

Three phases, in sequence. Skipping the first is what the gate exists to prevent.

Phase 0

Onboard

Research current professional standards for your domain on the live web before writing code. Record findings with real sources. Prefer primary and recent — the stack moves and training data goes stale.

Phase 1

Build

Implement, explicitly applying what Phase 0 established. Where research changed your approach, say so. Research that contradicts the brief is the most valuable kind.

Phase 2

Record

Write what you learned into your own skill file — a technique that failed, a constraint that wasn't obvious, a number you measured. Delete anything that stopped being true.

The gate. Nothing merges to master without its Phase 0 research recorded in the handbook. Ten research reports live in docs/research/ — one per discipline, each auditing the real code rather than summarising the web.

Why the skill files exist durable

  • Every engineer starts each assignment with no memory of the last one.
  • Knowledge that lives only in a finished conversation is knowledge the company buys twice.
  • Written for someone competent who has never seen this repo — a measured ratio beats "check contrast".

Integration discipline measured

  • Branches land one at a time, on arrival — never batched.
  • Every branch in the 14 Aug round needed Lead work to be shippable, and none of it showed up inside the branch. It was all seam damage.
  • CI runs E2E against a production build, because next dev compiles per request and times specs out under parallel workers.

A Data Access Layer, not a proxy

  • There is no proxy.ts. The DAL isn't a second layer behind one — it is the only layer, which is why it's authoritative rather than optimistic.
  • Next's own guidance: proxy "should not be your only line of defense" — it runs on prefetches, and a matcher edit can silently drop a route.
  • verifySessionCookie(cookie, true) is the revocation-checking form; cache() pays for the round trip once per render pass.

Security Rules emulator-tested

  • Default-deny, owner-only reads, no client writes at all. Every write goes through the Admin SDK, which bypasses rules by design.
  • Ownership is keyed by the path, not a field — a field can be spoofed on the write that creates it; a path segment compared against request.auth.uid cannot.
  • 13 assertions against the real emulator, on their own CI job. Both directions checked by injecting the two classic mistakes and watching the suite fail.
  • App Check is wired (reCAPTCHA v3) but enforcement is still an unclicked console action.

Contrast is computed, not eyeballed

  • src/lib/accent.ts searches outward from a brand's colour in both directions and takes the first variant clearing 3:1 — the smallest correction that works.
  • Not directional by assumption: on a light ground, bright accents fail and lightening makes it worse. Oraimo tops out at 2.21:1 even at pure white.
  • A border has two neighbours, so it's checked against both. Halotel passes on the page at 3.19:1 and fails inside its own chip at 2.75:1.
  • Brand colours are identity, so four of six brands come back untouched.

Imagery, and what we won't do

  • Brand logos are real, from Wikimedia Commons, attributed in SOURCES.md.
  • Product imagery is a custom SVG icon set — Commons searches for generic categories kept surfacing vintage or competitor-branded photos.
  • No scraped product photography, ever.
  • Motion respects prefers-reduced-motion, view transitions included — they run outside React where useReducedMotion() can't see them.

Nine things engineering can't decide

Each belongs to you, not an engineer. A number invented here would read as policy to whoever found it next.

Model

The bottom rung charges 25%

A 500 TZS fee on the cheapest product in the catalogue (TZS 2,000) is a quarter of it — the inherent cost of fixed fees per band. Accept it, add a narrower first rung, or set a minimum order value. This is the sharpest one left.

Model

Who verifies a branch really is that brand?

Nothing stops someone applying as "Toyota Arusha". The catalogue already separates researched fact from placeholder with the verified flag; partner approval needs the same discipline. This is an ops process, not a code change.

Model

Refund window, and who eats a refused delivery

How long can a customer cancel and still get the deposit back? And when someone refuses at the door, does the wasted trip get billed to them, absorbed by Soko, or borne by the courier?

Model

How long can a courier owe before losing jobs?

The 1,000 TZS is enforced by withholding work, which only works if the grace period is stated up front and visible on their page. And do already-accepted jobs still complete?

Model

Does the deposit apply to brand-fleet orders?

No courier means no delivery payment through Soko and no delivery rung, so the deposit would be the goods rung alone. Confirm that's intended — it's 6 of 19 branches today.

Debt #2

A git remote

Needs your GitHub account. Everything lives on one disk, and CI has never run because it has nowhere to run. Unblocks #1 and #11 together — and none of the work above should start on an unbacked-up disk.

Debt #20

A retention policy for customer PII

How long should an untouched saved request live, and should closing an account purge the subcollection? Customers can delete their own records now. The enforcement mechanism isn't built because the rule doesn't exist yet.

Debt #16

Turn on App Check enforcement

Wired in code, monitor-only in the console — Google's recommended sequence. Flipping it to enforcing is a click only you can make.
